Understanding the Problem
- Track product prices over time and notify users when a price crosses their chosen threshold.
- Discovery, trustworthy price collection, time-series storage, and notification fanout are separate parts of the pipeline.

Functional Requirements
- Show price history from a website or browser extension.
- Subscribe to price-drop notifications for a product and threshold.
- Continuously discover and refresh product prices.

Non-functional Requirements
- Scale to a catalog of around 500 million products without polling every product at the same high frequency.
- Return chart data quickly and avoid duplicate or misleading alerts.
- Treat extension-provided prices as untrusted observations, not automatically authoritative prices.

Core Entities
- Product identifies a marketplace item and canonical URL.
- PriceObservation records price, currency, timestamp, source, and validation state.
- Subscription links a user and product to a threshold and delivery preferences.
- CrawlJob and Notification track scheduled collection and retry-safe delivery.

API
- Query product history over a time range and requested chart resolution.
- Create, update, or remove a threshold subscription.
- Submit extension observations with product identity and evidence for validation.
- Return subscription state and distinguish accepted observations from verified prices.

High-Level Design

1) History and collection
- Collect prices through permitted source interfaces, scheduled fetches, and extension observations.
- Normalize product identity, currency, and timestamps before storing a time series.
- Index history by product and time; serve a bounded number of chart points rather than every raw observation.

Process: Collect and verify a price observation
- Step 1: A scheduler, source interface, or browser extension supplies a product observation with price, currency, time, and provenance.
- Step 2: The ingestion service normalizes product identity and validates the format, rate limits, and plausible value range.
- Step 3: Untrusted or anomalous observations enter verification; they do not immediately replace the authoritative price or trigger alerts.
- Step 4: The service persists a verified observation and compares it with the previous accepted price.
- Step 5: A real change produces a durable price-change event for history projections and subscription evaluation; duplicates retain the same event identity.

2) Subscriptions and alerts
- Store subscriptions indexed by product and threshold so a price change can locate eligible subscribers.
- Publish verified price-change events and process notification work asynchronously.
- Define whether an alert fires on crossing the threshold, every lower price, or once until rearmed.

Deep Dives

1) Discover and refresh products
- A naive full-catalog crawl wastes work on unpopular products whose prices rarely change.
- Prioritize tracked products, recently active products, and those with frequent price changes.
- Use user activity and extension observations as discovery signals, then schedule authoritative verification where needed.
- Keep per-source limits, retry backoff, and crawl state so failures do not restart the entire catalog scan.
- Partition collection tasks by product or source while avoiding concentrated load on one publisher endpoint.

2) Validate malicious observations
- Authenticate clients where possible, rate-limit submissions, and compare observations with historical ranges and independent sources.
- A single extreme report should trigger verification rather than immediately notify thousands of subscribers.
- Store provenance and confidence so suspicious data can be corrected without losing the audit trail.

3) Notify efficiently
- For a new price, query subscriptions whose threshold is satisfied using an indexed access path.
- Batch recipient work and enqueue delivery jobs so a popular product does not block price ingestion.
- Deduplicate by subscription and qualifying price event, then honor current notification preferences at delivery time.
- Retry provider failures with backoff and retain a terminal failure state for inspection.

Process: Send a threshold-crossing alert
- Step 1: A consumer receives a verified price-change event and looks up subscriptions for that product whose thresholds may be satisfied.
- Step 2: It checks each subscription's alert semantics, such as crossing from above the threshold to at or below it.
- Step 3: It records a deduplicated notification intent keyed by subscription and qualifying event, then reliably enqueues delivery work.
- Step 4: A delivery worker rechecks active preferences, sends through the chosen provider, and records the attempt outcome.
- Step 5: Transient failures retry with the same logical notification identity; already applied events do not generate another alert merely because queue delivery repeated.

4) Fast historical charts
- Partition time-series data by product and time to keep range scans bounded.
- Precompute hourly or daily aggregates for long ranges, retaining recent detailed samples where useful.
- Cache popular product/range combinations and ensure the resolution matches the displayed chart width.
- Preserve meaningful minima and maxima when downsampling so a short-lived price drop is not erased.

Process: Return chart data at a useful resolution
- Step 1: The client requests a product, time range, and desired chart resolution.
- Step 2: The query service checks a compatible cached result, then selects raw data or an appropriate rollup for that range.
- Step 3: It reads bounded product/time partitions and preserves the aggregates needed to represent meaningful price changes.
- Step 4: It returns ordered points and resolution metadata; the client renders the chart without downloading every raw observation.

Final Design
- Adaptive collection and validation produce trustworthy price-change events.
- Time-series storage and downsampled views serve history; indexed subscriptions drive asynchronous alerts.
- Provenance, deduplication, and explicit alert semantics prevent noisy collection from becoming misleading notifications.
