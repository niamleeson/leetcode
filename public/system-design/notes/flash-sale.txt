Understanding the Problem
- Sell a very limited inventory item when a large crowd arrives simultaneously.
- The critical invariant is that successful reservations and purchases never exceed available units.

Functional Requirements
- View the sale item and sale timing.
- Reserve an available unit for a limited checkout period.
- Complete payment for a valid reservation and release expired or abandoned claims.

Non-functional Requirements
- Preserve inventory consistency under extreme contention.
- Keep admission and reservation feedback responsive during traffic spikes.
- Recover payment and reservation failures without double-selling or holding inventory forever.

Core Entities
- SaleItem stores product metadata, sale window, and inventory definition.
- Reservation records unit or quantity, owner, expiration, token/version, and state.
- Order and PaymentAttempt track checkout and external payment outcomes.
- AdmissionToken controls entry from the waiting room into the reservation path.

API
- Fetch cached sale details and approximate availability.
- Request a reservation with an idempotency key and valid admission token where required.
- Complete checkout for a specific reservation token and query order status.
- Verify server time, ownership, sale state, and reservation validity on every authoritative transition.

High-Level Design


1) Browse and reserve
- Serve stable product details through caches or a CDN so browsers do not overload inventory storage.
- Claim inventory with an atomic conditional update or transaction.
- Persist a reservation with an explicit expiry instead of holding a database lock while the customer enters payment information.
- Cached availability is advisory; only a successful reservation grants a temporary claim.

2) Complete payment
- Coordinate payment through durable order state and stable provider idempotency keys.
- Convert the current reservation to purchased only through a valid conditional transition.
- Handle payment success racing reservation expiry explicitly, using a defined confirmation, reconciliation, or compensation policy.
- Notify the client asynchronously and allow status recovery after a lost response.

Deep Dives


1) Contention
- A single inventory counter becomes a serialization hot spot even if the rest of the database is sharded.
- Redis atomic coordination can reduce latency, but durable purchase correctness must survive its failure and failover behavior.
- Represent inventory as independently claimable units or buckets to spread contention, then atomically claim one eligible unit.
- Include ownership and version checks so a stale client cannot purchase a unit that has already been reassigned.
- Retry a bounded set of candidates; uncontrolled retries simply turn contention into more load.

2) Expiration
- Scheduled cleanup jobs are useful, but delays must not make expired inventory permanently unavailable.
- Reclaim expired reservations during a new claim using an atomic predicate on state, expiration, and version.
- A delayed cleanup task must verify that it still refers to the same reservation before releasing anything.
- Define expiry against authoritative server time and keep terminal purchased state immune to reservation cleanup.

3) Crowd admission
- A basic rate limit protects the backend but gives customers repeated errors and no clear place in line.
- A virtual waiting room issues signed, expiring admission tokens at a sustainable rate.
- Keep queue state independent of inventory claims; admission does not guarantee a unit.
- Bound token reuse and recover reconnecting users without letting refreshes jump the queue.

4) Fairness
- Define whether admission is first-come-first-served, randomized, or another transparent policy.
- Enforce per-user purchase limits at the durable order boundary, not solely in browser state.
- Separate fairness from raw throughput: a fast atomic counter does not prevent one client from making disproportionate attempts.

Final Design
- Cached browsing and a waiting room control the arrival rate at a short atomic reservation path.
- Versioned, expiring unit claims spread contention and prevent stale checkout actions.
- Durable payment workflows and conditional completion preserve the inventory invariant through retries, timeouts, and cleanup.
